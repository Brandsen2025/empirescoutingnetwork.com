📋 HISTORICAL VALIDATION PILOT — RETROACTIVE ASSESSMENT
This card applies the Empire FGA v3 video scoring methodology retroactively to archival footage of the 1996–97 season. It serves two functions: (1) validating that the FGA framework produces defensible scores for a historically elite player, and (2) establishing an ERA-adjusted baseline for cross-era comparison. The Halo Effect Rule applies unconditionally — every score derives from what the video shows, not from Ronaldo's reputation. Where the footage produces a 5/10 DMA event, it is recorded as 5/10. The standard is the rubric, not the name.
⚖️ HALO EFFECT RULE — APPLIED
Two DMA events scored 5/10 ("one touch pass unsuccessful" M2 7:12 · "hesitating to make a choice, ball lost" M1 87:53). Three offsides in M1 logged in PUNCH_LIST — OBI_T at 9.33 reflects the frequency and precision of his off-ball runs, not their legal outcome. A run that is one centimetre offside is mechanically identical to a run that is two centimetres onside. The rubric scores movement quality. The referee scores legality.

Career Timeline
Born Sep 22, 1976 · Belo Horizonte, Brazil
Grew up in Itaguaí, then Belo Horizonte. Joined Cruzeiro youth at 14. Brazilian football context — the formation ground of the most technically gifted generation in football history.
1993–94 · Cruzeiro · Age 16–17
Professional debut at 16 with Cruzeiro Esporte Clube in Série A. Scored 12 goals in 14 games. Included in Brazil's 1994 World Cup squad by Parreira but did not play.
1994–95 · PSV Eindhoven · Age 17–18
First European club · Eredivisie. Sold to PSV for €6M. 30 goals in 33 Eredivisie matches. Reveals the full Ronaldo model to Europe — acceleration, dribbling, finishing. World Under-17 tournament star the previous year had made him one of the most watched teenagers in world football.
1995–96 · FC Barcelona · Age 18–19
La Liga, Copa del Rey, Copa UEFA. Transferred for a then world-record fee. First season: immediate impact, won Copa del Rey and Copa UEFA. Established as the best player on the planet at age 19.
1996–97 · FC Barcelona · The Assessed Season
34 goals in 37 La Liga matches. 5 hat-tricks. FIFA World Player of the Year. This is the season assessed in this card. The matches covered span the full range of his La Liga campaign — from bottom-third Logroñes to Real Madrid and Valencia in title-race context.
1997–2002 · Inter Milan · Injury period
World record transfer to Inter (€27M). Devastating knee injuries in 1999 and 2000 cost him three years. Returns for the 2002 World Cup in a different physical state — equally effective through different means.
2002 · Brazil · World Cup · The Redemption
8 goals · 2 in the final · Golden Boot · World Cup winner. The pre-injury Ronaldo of 1996–97 and the post-injury Ronaldo of 2002 are the same player at different physical states. This assessment covers the former — O Fenômeno at his physical peak.

Full Historical Assessment Report
Jim Totime · Apr 2026 · 7 matches · La Liga 1996–97 · ERA ×0.95
This assessment covers Luiz Nazário de Lima, known as Ronaldo or O Fenômeno, at FC Barcelona in the 1996–97 La Liga season. He was 19–20 years old. The seven matches analysed include home wins against Atlético Madrid, Real Madrid, Valencia CF, and Logroñes; away wins at Zaragoza; a home loss at Tenerife; and an away draw vs Fiorentina. The protocol passes all four balance checks. The ERA adjustment of ×0.95 is applied to the final composite due to fixed-camera footage limitations and one known weather condition event (M3 Zaragoza: 32°C / 70% humidity).
SII_A 9.36 across 31 confirmed events is the highest event-count and highest average score for any single metric across all 11 assessments in this series. Thirty-one separate acceleration events, scored individually across seven matches, averaging above 9.0 at La Liga level against Serie A and La Liga defenders. The floor event is a 8/10. There are thirteen 10/10 perfect events within those 31. The 10/10 at M4 15:54 describes him passing between two defenders before beating Zubizarreta. The 10/10 at M4 77:39 describes the Valencia goal — recovery at 40 metres, carriers past two defenders in tight space. The 10/10 at M3 93:36 describes a run while 90+3 minutes into a 0-0 La Liga away match — he has recovered from the full game and is still accelerating at maximum output.
SOA 9.27 across 26 events (26 events — another all-series record) confirms that the acceleration (SII_A) is matched by technical quality on the ball during those accelerations. The sole dribble at M3 89:04 scores 10/10 simultaneously as SOA and CRK — "1v1 successful by step-over amazingly fast." The M1 62:09 event — "receives ball, turns to start run with 360 spin eliminating first opponent, ball carries to dribble two opponents, 1v1 successful before pass to Pizzi" — scores SOA 10, SII_A 9.5, CRK 10, DYN 10, MCS 9.5 simultaneously. The most complex technical-physical combined event in the M1 sample.
The Valencia Goal (M4 77:40) requires its own paragraph. "His most famous goal with Barcelona: recovers ball at 40 metres, tremendous carries, passes between Ezkurza and Otero in a thin space before beating Zubizarreta." This action simultaneously triggered 10/10 on SII_A, DMA, CHR, MCS, SPI, CRS, DYN, and CRK. Eight separate video metrics, each assessed independently, all scoring at the ceiling of the rubric on the same action. No player across any other card in this series has produced more than four simultaneous elite scores. Ronaldo produces eight. This single timestamp is the clearest possible illustration of why the FGA methodology exists: the Tier 1 data records it as 1 goal. The video records it as eight 10/10 quality signals.
MCS 8.43 across 8 events is the mental framework evidence. The distribution includes: 6/10 at M1 (López and Prodan kicking him repeatedly — he asks the referee to intervene), 9/10 at M2 54:51 (scores from rebound after missing a penalty), 10/10 at M3 67:51 (dribbles 4 defenders at 35 metres from goal), 9/10 at M1 62:13 (same action as the 360 spin), 10/10 at M4 77:42 (Valencia goal), 9/10 at M1 75:48 (hat-trick goal under pressure). The 6/10 at M1 is the honest floor — it describes a legitimate moment of frustration. What follows in the same match is a 9/10 MCS and then a hat-trick. The MCS 8.43 average is accurate: elite competitive mindset with visible but non-destructive response to extreme provocation.
Two DMA events at 5/10 are documented honestly. M2 7:12: "one touch pass unsuccessful." M1 87:53: "hesitating to make a choice and ball lost finally." The DMA 8.0 average (11 events) absorbs both of these. The floor is 5/10. The ceiling is 10/10 (M3 67:50 · 4-defender dribble at 35 metres). A 19-year-old striker in La Liga will make wrong decisions. The methodology requires logging them. They are in the data.
