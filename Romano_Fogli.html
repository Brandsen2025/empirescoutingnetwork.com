Profile

Romano Fogli, nicknamed "Il Grissino" (The Breadstick) for his slight 1.72m / 69kg frame, was a technically gifted deep-lying playmaker / box-to-box midfielder for Torino, Bologna, AC Milan and Catania between 1955 and 1974. A Serie A champion with Bologna (1963-64) and European Cup / Intercontinental Cup winner with AC Milan (1969), he won 13 caps for Italy including the 1966 World Cup. He died on 21 September 2021, aged 83.

"Il calcio italiano esprimeva quel giorno di dicembre del 1958 soltanto Romano Fogli, che sta a Corso e a Rivera come un caposcuola. Tecnica pura, ai limiti del lirismo, la sua. Il football di casa nostra partoriva il primo 'enfant prodige', il capostipite degli 'abatini'…"
— Lino Cascioli, Il Calcio Illustrato, 26 June 1966 · "Il Caposcuola" (The Headmaster)

Fogli was seen as the pioneer of a new, technically gifted generation — the "caposcuola" (headmaster) who paved the way for Gianni Rivera and Mario Corso, combining tecnica pura (pure technique) with a willingness to sacrifice himself defensively.

"Fogli ha la classe innata di chi trova facilissime le cose difficili, di chi realizza l'impossibile. Abbiamo visto molti celeberrimi assi fare, davanti a lui, la figura dei principianti. Preferisce le mezzali tecniche, che accettano il confronto sul terreno delle reciproche finezze. Il suo gioco è miniato, cesellato."
— Gian Paolo Ormezzano, Lo Sport, 27 March 1958 · "Il Gioco Miniato" (The Miniature Game)

He was not a physical powerhouse (hence "Grissino"), but majestic in possession, using close control and precise passing to dictate tempo — "miniato, cesellato" (miniature, chiselled) captures the artistry of his game.

Historical Context & Legacy

Romano Fogli is the archetypal Italian technical midfielder of the 1960s: a player whose elegance, intelligence, and tactical versatility made him one of the most respected midfielders of his generation. Born in Santa Maria a Monte, he came through the Torino youth system before joining Bologna in 1958, where he became the heartbeat of the team that won the 1963-64 Serie A title.

His performance in the 1964 Scudetto playoff against Inter is part of Italian football folklore. Fogli scored the opening goal with a deflected free-kick and provided the assist for Nielsen's decisive second, earning Man of the Match honours — a performance that cemented his reputation as a big-game player.

He later moved to AC Milan, where he played a crucial role in their 1969 European Cup triumph. In the semi-final against Manchester United, he came off the bench to replace the injured Gianni Rivera and produced a colossal defensive midfield performance, nullifying Denis Law and driving Milan to victory. He also played in the Intercontinental Cup against Estudiantes.
