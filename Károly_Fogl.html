Profile

Károly Fogl ("Fogl II" — the second of three footballing brothers) was a right-back / centre-back who spent his career at Újpesti Törekvés SE (1912–1914), Újpest FC (1914–1930) as club captain, and Vasas SC (1931). He won 51 caps for Hungary, played at the 1924 Paris Olympics, and was named Hungarian Footballer of the Year in 1920. This is a historical archive file, not a scouting assessment.

Historical Context & Legacy

Károly Fogl is the archetypal Hungarian defender of the early 20th century: a player whose physical presence, tactical intelligence, and technical quality made him one of the most respected defenders of his era. Born in Újpest, he came through the local youth ranks before establishing himself as a cornerstone of the Újpest FC and Hungary national teams.

His partnership with his brother József — the "Diga-Fogl" — was celebrated across Europe as one of the finest defensive pairings of the 1920s. Together, they formed a wall that opposing forwards struggled to breach. Fogl was named Hungarian Footballer of the Year in 1920, and he represented his country 51 times, including at the 1924 Paris Olympics.

But his most significant contribution may have been tactical. In November 1918, he was deployed as a central defender in a back three with man-marking duties — a system that anticipated Herbert Chapman's WM formation by seven years. The source explicitly credits him with "anticipating the Sistema," making him a genuine pioneer of modern defensive football.

After retiring, he became a successful coach, managing Bulgaria, Sportklub Sofia, Warta Poznań, and Polonia Warsaw, winning a Polish title in 1947. He died in Budapest in 1969.
