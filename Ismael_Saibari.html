Ismael Saibari is a 25-year-old Moroccan attacking midfielder, signed by FC Bayern Munich from PSV Eindhoven on 1 July 2026 for a fee reported between €50M and €55M. Born 28 January 2001 in Terrassa, Spain, to a Moroccan family from Ksar el-Kebir; moved to Willebroek, Belgium at age six. 185cm, right-footed. Contract to 30 June 2031, keeps squad number 34. This report is built entirely from public statistical sources (FotMob, Wikipedia) and press coverage of his transfer and World Cup run — it is not a Tier 3 video-scouted assessment.
Career-best exit season — 15 goals, 8 assists, 2,145 Eredivisie minutes, 7.90 average rating (FotMob, 2025-26). Joined PSV in July 2020 from Genk's youth system, broke through with Jong PSV, made his Eredivisie debut 1 Nov 2020 as a sub for Mario Götze. Became a first-team fixture from 2023-24 (31 apps, 8 goals, Eredivisie title), stepped up further in 2024-25 (44 apps, 15G/13A, second title), and peaked in his final PSV season with 19 goals in all competitions and a first career hat-trick (26 Oct 2025, away at Feyenoord).
Shooting output — 91 shots, 15 goals from 12.95 xG (+2.05), 41% on target (FotMob). Overperforming expected goals by two full goals across a season is a real signal, but on its own it can't distinguish elite finishing from a run of good luck or shot-quality mismeasurement — that distinction is exactly what match-level video review would resolve, and hasn't been done here.
Creation and carrying — 52 chances created (11 "big"), 8 assists from 7.41 xA, 48 successful dribbles at 56.5%, 175 touches in the opposition box (FotMob). A high-involvement attacking profile: he's not just finishing, he's generating and carrying into the box in volume. 38 times dispossessed in the same sample says the carrying comes with turnover risk.
Defensive/duel numbers — 52 defensive contributions, 33 tackles, 47.3% duels won, 45.5% aerial (FotMob). Unremarkable for the position, not concerning on the numbers alone. Whether that's positional discipline or lack of defensive engagement needs footage, not box scores.
World Cup 2026 — genuine breakout, not hype. Scored the tournament's fastest goal (69 seconds vs Scotland, beating Ziyech's 4-minute mark from 2022), scored in all three Morocco group matches, and converted the winning penalty against the Netherlands to send Morocco into the round of 16 — drawing level with Youssef En-Nesyri as Morocco's all-time World Cup scorer. Multiple outlets (The National, beIN Sports, Al Jazeera) independently framed him as the tournament's breakthrough star ahead of the more-hyped Brahim Díaz.
Off-pitch note — AFCON 2025 final (18 Jan 2026). Saibari personally drew a 3-match suspension for an incident involving ball boys and Senegal's Édouard Mendy. Separately, Senegal's own walkout during stoppage time led CAF to later void their 1-0 win and award Morocco the title by forfeit (3-0), a ruling that landed 57 days after the match itself. Two distinct storylines worth keeping separate when reviewing this period's footage.
