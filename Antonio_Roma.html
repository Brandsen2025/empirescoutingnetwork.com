🌍 Empire FGA Analysis — The Acrobatic Cat

Antonio Roma represents the archetype of the classic South American showman-goalkeeper — a fearless shot-stopper with exceptional reflexes, acrobatic agility, and commanding presence who anchored Boca Juniors' golden era of the 1960s. His FGA rating of 88 places him in the Elite Goalkeeper tier — combining elite shot-stopping ability (REF 95), strong aerial command (AER 89), and exceptional bravery (BRA 96) that made him one of the most beloved keepers in Boca's history. Nicknamed "El Gato" (The Cat) for his feline agility and acrobatic saves, Roma was the last line of defense during Boca's three consecutive championships (1962, 1964, 1965) and the 1963 Copa Libertadores final run. Unlike the modern sweeper-keeper, Roma was a pure line-keeper who dominated his area through positional intelligence and sheer courage.

Roma played in the era of the "goalkeeper as spectacle" — his style was acrobatic and aggressive, often leaving his line to punch clear or claim crosses in crowds. In Pedernera's defensive system, he was protected by a backline featuring Rattín and Marzolini, allowing him to focus on shot-stopping rather than sweeping. 🌍 Career Journey — The Eternal Cat

Seventeen-year journey from Ferro youth to Boca legend and Pelé's nemesis.

1953-1955 — Ferrocarril Oeste
Began career at Ferrocarril Oeste in Buenos Aires. Showed promise as an acrobatic young keeper with exceptional reflexes. Played 23 matches before attracting Boca's attention with his penalty-saving prowess.
1956-1972 — Boca Juniors (Glory)
The golden era. Signed by Boca in 1956, became first-choice keeper by 1958. Won three consecutive championships (1962, 1964, 1965) under Adolfo Pedernera. Reached 1963 Copa Libertadores final, saving penalty from Pelé. Made 533 appearances, keeping 236 clean sheets. Retired in 1972 as a club icon.
1956-1967 — Argentina National Team
Earned 42 caps for Argentina, serving as first-choice goalkeeper during the late 1950s and early 1960s. Played in the 1957 Copa América (won by Argentina) and 1958 World Cup in Sweden. Known for his penalty saves in international friendlies against Brazil.
The Pelé Rivalry: Roma faced Pelé's Santos multiple times between 1962-1964. In the 1963 Libertadores final first leg, he saved Pelé's penalty at La Bombonera. In 1964, he saved another Pelé penalty in a friendly. This psychological dominance over the world's greatest player elevated Roma to mythical status among Boca fans. Pelé himself later remarked that Roma was "the most difficult goalkeeper I ever faced in South America."
